Wikollector Privacy Policy

Last updated 10 November 2026

Wikollector is a card-collecting game made by an independent developer, Joseph Rogers ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the game uses, why, and the choices you have. If you have a question, email wikollector.support@gmail.com.

What the game collects

  • Your game account. When you first play, the game creates an anonymous account so your progress is saved. If you choose Sign in with Apple, I receive the identifier Apple gives for your account and the email Apple shares (which can be a private relay address if you choose "Hide My Email"). I don't receive your name or your Apple password.
  • Game progress. Your cards, Gold, Gems, Energy, trades, market listings, match results, wishlist, watchlist and settings, so the game works and your save follows you to a new phone.
  • Things you share with other players. Your player handle, friends list, forum posts and shared pulls. Other players can see your handle, your posts and anything you list on the market.
  • Notifications. If you allow notifications, a push token so the game can tell you about things like card alerts. You can turn them off in your phone's Settings at any time.
  • Purchases. Purchases are handled by Apple. I never see your card or payment details. RevenueCat, the service that checks purchases with Apple, receives what you bought along with your game account ID, so the right Gems and perks reach your account.
  • Ads. The game shows an ad only when you choose to watch one to double a reward. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, which may collect device information, your IP address, an approximate location worked out from your IP address, ad performance data, crash and diagnostic data, and (only if you allow tracking when your phone asks) your device's advertising identifier. I record that you finished an ad so the reward can be paid.
  • Security check. When the game creates your account or you sign in, Cloudflare Turnstile runs an invisible check to confirm a real player, not a bot, is signing in. Cloudflare may collect information about your device and connection for this, as described in the Cloudflare Turnstile Privacy Addendum.
  • Crash and error reports. If the game crashes or something important fails (for example a card pull that gets no answer), it sends a report to Sentry so I can fix it. A report includes what went wrong, the app and game version, your phone model and iOS version, and your game account ID (a random code, not your name or email). It doesn't include your IP address, contacts or anything you type. Reports are deleted automatically after 90 days at most.
  • Technical data. My servers (run by Supabase) keep short-lived logs, such as IP addresses and error messages, to keep the game running and secure.

The game never asks for your precise (GPS) location, contacts, photos or microphone, and I don't sell your information.

Why it's used

To run the game and save your progress (performing our agreement with you); to deliver what you buy; to keep the game fair and secure, for example preventing cheating and abuse (legitimate interests); and to show ads, with personalised ads only where you've consented.

Services I use

  • Supabase: hosting the game's servers and database
  • RevenueCat: checking in-app purchases
  • Google AdMob: rewarded ads; see also how Google uses data from apps
  • Cloudflare Turnstile: the invisible bot check at sign-in (see the Turnstile Privacy Addendum)
  • Sentry: crash and error reports
  • Expo: building the app and delivering game updates to your phone
  • Apple: Sign in with Apple, payments and push notifications
  • Wikipedia (Wikimedia Foundation): card content comes from Wikipedia, so your phone may load articles and images from Wikimedia's servers

I only share data with these services so they can do the jobs listed above, and each of them provides the same or equal protection of your data as this policy describes. Some of them process data outside the UK. Where they do, they use safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

Your ad choices

In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before showing personalised ads. You can change your choice at any time in Profile → Ad privacy choices. On iPhone you can also turn off tracking in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. If you buy ad-free doubles, the game stops showing ads.

If you live in California or another US state with a similar law, allowing personalised ads may count as "sharing" your information for advertising. You can opt out by leaving tracking off in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, or by turning on Limit Ad Tracking where your phone offers it, and Google will then show only non-personalised ads.

How long it's kept, and deleting your account

Your game data is kept while you have an account. You can delete your account and everything stored with it at any time in Profile → Delete account. Forum posts are deleted with it. Records needed for tax or accounting, such as purchase records, may be kept for as long as the law requires. Server logs are deleted automatically after a short period, and crash reports after 90 days at most.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to access, correct, delete or move your data, and object to or restrict how it's used. Email wikollector.support@gmail.com and I'll reply within a month. If you're unhappy with how I've handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Children

Wikollector isn't aimed at children under 13, and I don't knowingly collect their data. If you think a child under 13 has an account, email me and I'll delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, I'll update it here and change the date at the top. For big changes I'll also let players know in the game.
